4 Analysis on triangles

Lemma 4.1 Geometry of admissible meshes
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.3

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.MeshOK
  • ABCM.normals_eq_outwardNormal
  • ABCM.cellFace
  • ABCM.two_mul_le_height
  • ABCM.height_le_cellSize

An admissible mesh is a conforming shape-regular simplicial mesh; its stored normals are the unit outward normals of the triangles, every side of a triangle belongs to a unique edge, and every height \(H_j\) of a triangle satisfies \(2\rho _K\le H_j\le h_K\).

Proof ▶

Elementary simplex geometry, using the affine-simplex library of Mathlib.

Lemma 4.2 Divergence theorem and cone formula on a simplex
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.integral_fderiv_eq_sum_facets
  • ABCM.integral_closedInterior_cone

For a full-dimensional simplex \(s\) with facets \(F_j\) and unit outward normals \(\nu _j\), every \(C^1\) function \(g\) and every direction \(w\): \(\int _s\nabla g\cdot w=\sum _j(w\cdot \nu _j)\int _{F_j}g\). Integrals over \(s\) can be computed as cone integrals over a facet.

Proof ▶

The cone formula \(\int _sf=H_i\int _0^1t^n\int _{F_i}f(v_i+t(y-v_i))\, \mathrm{d}y\, \mathrm{d}t\) reduces the radial derivative to facet integrals; combining the radial identities at two vertices gives every direction, since the edge vectors span the space.

Lemma 4.3 Density of smooth functions
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.1

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.exists_smooth_approx

On an open convex set containing a ball, every weak jet of order \(m\) is the \(H^m\) limit of the jets of smooth functions.

Proof ▶

Dilate towards the centre of the ball, which maps the closure into the set, and mollify.

Lemma 4.4 Sobolev traces
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 4.3
  • Lemma 4.2
  • Lemma 4.1
  • Definition 2.4

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.smooth_trace_ineq
  • ABCM.hasSobolevTrace_trace_ineq
  • ABCM.exists_hasSobolevTrace
  • ABCM.traceRealizationExists

For \(C^1\) functions on a shape-regular cell, \(h_K\lVert w\rVert _{L^2(F_j)}^2\le \frac\rho 2\bigl((d+1)\lVert w\rVert _{L^2(K)}^2+h_K^2\lVert \nabla w\rVert _{L^2(K)}^2\bigr)\). Every weak jet of order \(m\) on a cell has Sobolev traces, and every jet of order \(k\ge 1\) on the domain has a broken realization with Sobolev traces on every admissible mesh.

Proof ▶

The trace inequality follows from the radial identity of Lemma 4.2. For the traces, approximate by smooth functions (Lemma 4.3); the trace inequality makes the facet restrictions Cauchy in \(L^2\).

Lemma 4.5 Green’s formula for jets with traces
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 4.4
  • Lemma 4.2

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.hasSobolevTrace_green

If \(J\) has Sobolev traces \(t\) of order \(m\) on a cell \(T\) and \(\lvert \alpha \rvert {\lt}m\), then for every \(C^1\) function \(v\) and every direction \(a\): \(\int _T(a\cdot \nabla J_\alpha )v+\int _TJ_\alpha \, a\cdot \nabla v=\sum _j(a\cdot n_j)\int _{F_j} t_j^\alpha v\).

Proof ▶

Lemma 4.2 for smooth approximants and passage to the limit.

Lemma 4.6 Traces across interior edges
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 4.5

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.traceAgreement
  • ABCM.jump_eq_zero_of_realizes
  • ABCM.trace_eq_zero_of_inH01

If \(J\) is a weak jet of order \(m\) on the domain with Sobolev traces on every cell, then for \(\lvert \alpha \rvert {\lt}m\) the traces from the two sides of an interior edge agree almost everywhere. In particular \(H^2\cap H^1_0\) fields have zero jumps on all edges, and traces of \(H^1_0\) jets vanish on boundary edges.

Proof ▶

Green’s formula on both cells and the weak derivative on the domain give \(\int _e(t_T-t_S)\psi =0\) for all smooth \(\psi \) supported near the open edge; the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations on the edge gives \(t_T=t_S\) almost everywhere.

Lemma 4.7 Inverse and discrete trace inequalities
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 4.1
  • Lemma 4.2

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.exists_cell_inverse
  • ABCM.exists_cell_inverse_H2
  • ABCM.exists_cell_trace

For polynomials \(q\) of degree at most \(k\) on the cells of admissible meshes with shape bound \(\rho \): \(h_K^2\lVert \partial _iq\rVert _K^2\le C\lVert q\rVert _K^2\) (and the analogous \(H^2\)–\(H^1\) bound), and \(h_K\lVert q\rVert _{F_j}^2\le C\lVert q\rVert _K^2\), with \(C=C(d,k,\rho )\).

Proof ▶

Equivalence of norms on polynomials of bounded degree on the unit ball, transported by the affine maps between an inscribed and a circumscribed ball; the trace bound uses the radial identity.

Lemma 4.8 Poincaré–Wirtinger on convex sets
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.poincare_convex

For a compact convex set \(K\) of diameter at most \(h\) and \(u\in C^1\): \(\lvert K\rvert \int _Ku^2-(\int _Ku)^2\le 2^{d+1}h^2\lvert K\rvert \int _K\lvert \nabla u\rvert ^2\).

Proof ▶

Split \(u(x)-u(y)\) at the midpoint of \([x,y]\) and write each half as a segment integral of \(\nabla u\) along a dilation towards one end point.

Lemma 4.9 Bramble–Hilbert
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 4.8
  • Lemma 4.3

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.exists_poly_moments
  • ABCM.exists_brambleHilbert

For a weak jet \(J\) of order \(k+1\) on a cell \(T\) of an admissible mesh there is a polynomial \(q\) of degree at most \(k\) with \(h_T^{2j}\lvert J-q\rvert _{H^j(T)}^2\le Ch_T^{2(k+1)}\lvert J\rvert _{H^{k+1}(T)}^2\) for all \(j\le k+1\), with \(C=C(d,k,\rho )\).

Proof ▶

Choose \(q\) with \(\int _T\partial ^\alpha (J-q)=0\) for \(\lvert \alpha \rvert \le k\) (polynomials with prescribed moments of all derivatives) and apply Lemma 4.8 repeatedly, first to smooth approximants.

Lemma 4.10 Scaled Sobolev sup bound
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 4.1

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.exists_sobolev_sup

For \(d\le 3\), smooth \(u\) and every point \(x\) of a cell \(T\): \(\lvert T\rvert u(x)^2\le C\bigl(\lVert u\rVert _T^2+h_T^2\lvert u\rvert _{H^1(T)}^2+h_T^4\lvert u\rvert _{H^2(T)}^2\bigr)\), with \(C=C(d)\).

Proof ▶

Taylor’s formula along segments from \(x\), averaged over \(y\in T\), with the remainder controlled in \(L^2\); the restriction \(d\le 3\) makes the resulting weight integrable.
