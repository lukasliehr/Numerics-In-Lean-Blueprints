5 The Dirichlet problem on a convex polygon

Lemma 5.1 Weak solutions
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.2

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.laxMilgram_existsUnique
  • ABCM.poincare_H01
  • ABCM.existsUnique_weakSolution

A coercive bounded bilinear form on a Hilbert space has a unique solution for every bounded functional. If \(U\) lies in a slab of width \(L\), then \(\lVert v\rVert \le L\lVert \partial _{i_0}v\rVert \) on \(H^1_0(U)\). Consequently, on a bounded set, \(-\Delta u=f\) has a weak solution in \(H^1_0\), unique up to its derivatives of order at most one.

Proof ▶

\(H^1_0(U)\) is the closure of the graphs of test functions in \(L^2(U)^{1+d}\), hence complete; the Poincaré inequality gives coercivity of \((\nabla u,\nabla v)\).

Lemma 5.2 Regularity on translation-invariant domains
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 5.1

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.exists_dir_deriv
  • ABCM.exists_hessian_transInv

For a coercive constant-coefficient problem on a domain invariant under translations along \(a\), the solution has weak derivatives along \(a\) in \(H^1_0\), bounded by the load. In particular, on the plane and on half-planes, \(v\in H^1_0\) with \((\nabla v,\nabla \varphi )=(F,\varphi )\) has weak second derivatives with \(\lVert \partial _{ij}v\rVert \le 11\lVert F\rVert +10\lVert v\rVert _{H^1}\).

Proof ▶

Difference quotients along \(a\) preserve \(H^1_0\) and solve the same problem with a differenced load; they are bounded and converge. The normal second derivative is recovered from the equation.

Lemma 5.3 Sharp Wirtinger inequality
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 5.1

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.wirtinger_H01

On the strip \(Q=\{ 0{\lt}\theta {\lt}\omega \} \), every \(v\in H^1_0(Q)\) satisfies \((\pi /\omega )\lVert v\rVert \le \lVert \partial _\theta v\rVert \).

Proof ▶

For test functions, \((\partial _\theta \varphi )^2-\nu ^2\varphi ^2=\partial _\theta (\nu \cot (\nu \theta ) \varphi ^2)+(\partial _\theta \varphi -\nu \cot (\nu \theta )\varphi )^2\) with \(\nu =\pi /\omega \); integrate and pass to the closure.

Lemma 5.4 The shifted strip problem
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 5.2
  • Lemma 5.3

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.shifted_equation
  • ABCM.exists_strip_hessian

If \(W\in H^1_0(Q)\) solves \((\nabla W,\nabla w)=(e^tf,w)\) and \(z=e^{-t}W\in H^1_0(Q)\), then \(z\) solves a coercive, translation-invariant shifted problem; hence \(\partial _tz\in H^1_0(Q)\) and \(\partial _\theta ^2z\in L^2(Q)\), with bounds by \(\lVert f\rVert \).

Proof ▶

Coercivity uses the sharp Wirtinger inequality (\(\pi /\omega {\gt}1\)); then Lemma 5.2 in the direction \(t\), and the equation for the remaining derivative.

Lemma 5.5 Regularity at a convex corner
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 5.4
  • Lemma 5.3

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.Frame.sector_hessian

On a sector of opening \(0{\lt}\omega {\lt}\pi \), a solution \(v\in H^1_0\) of \((\nabla v,\nabla \varphi )=(F,\varphi )\) with \(F\) vanishing outside a ball around the apex has weak second derivatives with \(\lVert \partial _{ij}v\rVert \le C(\omega )\lVert F\rVert \).

Proof ▶

Polar coordinates \(t=\log r\) map the sector conformally onto the strip \(Q\). The transported function \(W\) lies in \(H^1_0(Q)\) (conformal invariance of the Dirichlet energy and the Wirtinger inequality), and \(z=e^{-t}W\in H^1_0(Q)\) by an energy estimate with truncated exponential weights. Lemma 5.4 gives \(H^2\) regularity of \(z\), which transfers back to the sector by the chain rule for test functions.

Lemma 5.6 Local geometry of convex polygons
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 5.2
  • Lemma 5.5

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.cone_classify
  • ABCM.cone_eq_sector
  • ABCM.ModelReg
  • ABCM.polygon_local

A nonempty finite intersection of open half-planes through \(0\) is the plane, a half-plane or a convex sector. Hence around every point of \(\overline\Omega \) there are a model domain \(V\supseteq \Omega \) and \(\delta {\gt}0\) with \(V\cap B(x,\delta )\subseteq \Omega \), and every model domain satisfies the local \(H^2\) estimate.

Proof ▶

Classification of tangent cones; the plane and half-planes satisfy the model estimate by Lemma 5.2, and convex sectors by Lemma 5.5.

Lemma 5.7 Localization
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 5.6

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.local_hessian

If \(U\subseteq V\), \(V\cap B(x_0,\delta )\subseteq U\), \(V\) satisfies the model estimate at \(x_0\) and the cutoff \(c\) is supported in \(B(x_0,\delta )\), then \(cv\) has an \(L^2\) Hessian for every solution \(v\), bounded by \(C(\lVert F\rVert +\lVert v\rVert )\).

Proof ▶

\(cv\) solves the same equation with load \(cF-\sum _i(2\partial _ic\, \partial _iv+\partial _i^2c\, v)\).

Theorem 5.8 \(H^2\) regularity on convex polygons
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 5.7
  • Lemma 5.6
  • Lemma 5.1

Lean declarations

  • ABCM.polygon_hessian

On a convex polygon there is \(C\) such that every solution \(v\in H^1_0(\Omega )\) of \((\nabla v,\nabla \varphi )=(F,\varphi )\) has weak second derivatives bounded by \(C(\lVert F\rVert +\lVert v\rVert )\).

Proof ▶

A smooth partition of unity near \(\overline\Omega \) subordinate to the neighbourhoods of Lemma 5.6 reduces the estimate to Lemma 5.7.
