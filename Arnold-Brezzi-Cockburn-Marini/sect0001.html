1 Overview

This blueprint describes a complete Lean 4 formalization of the main results of

D. N. Arnold, F. Brezzi, B. Cockburn, L. D. Marini, Unified analysis of discontinuous Galerkin methods for elliptic problems, SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 39 (2002), no. 5, 1749–1779.

The model problem is \(-\Delta u=f\) in a bounded open convex polygon \(\Omega \subset \mathbb {R}^2\), \(u=0\) on \(\partial \Omega \). The paper writes discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods in a unified flux formulation, eliminates the auxiliary variable to obtain primal forms, and analyses nine methods (Table 6.1 of the paper): the four consistent, adjoint-consistent and stable methods (interior penalty, Bassi et al., Brezzi et al., LDG), with optimal error estimates; ordinary NIPG and the two pure penalty methods; the superpenalized variants; the Baumann–Oden method; and the original Bassi–Rebay method, which is only weakly stable.

The Lean files.

  • Showcase.lean imports only Mathlib. It defines the setting (weak derivatives, triangulations, broken fields with traces, liftings, the forms of all methods, norms and numerical fluxes) and states eight theorems in the paper’s notation, with sorry.

  • Showcase_WithProofs.lean proves the same theorems from the library LeanCode.

  • comparator.json configures lake comparator. It checks that the proofs establish exactly the statements of Showcase.lean and use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.

  • The library LeanCode contains about 31 000 lines of proofs, organized as in the chapters below. LeanCode/Definitions.lean repeats the definitions of Showcase.lean verbatim; LeanCode/Main/Bundled.lean states the results in bundled form and LeanCode/Main/Proofs.lean derives the eight theorems from them.

  • blueprint/DetailedProofs.pdf contains detailed mathematical proofs of these results. In places the Lean proofs take a different route, notably for the \(H^2\) regularity on convex polygons, which is proved here without the Fourier transform.

Scope.

Every theorem holds for every polynomial degree \(p\ge 1\) (for Baumann–Oden \(p\ge 2\), with a counterexample for \(p=1\)) and every shape bound \(\kappa \); meshes are conforming triangulations of \(\Omega \) with \(h_K/\rho _K\le \kappa \), without quasi-uniformity. All constants are chosen before the mesh, the edge coefficients, the load and the exact solution.
