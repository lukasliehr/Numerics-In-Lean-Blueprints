A jet on \(\mathbb {R}^d\) assigns a function \(J_\alpha \) to every multi-index \(\alpha \); the jet of a smooth function \(u\) is \(\alpha \mapsto \partial ^\alpha u\). A jet is a weak jet of order \(m\) on an open set \(U\) if \(J_\alpha \in L^2(U)\) for \(\lvert \alpha \rvert \le m\) and \(J_{\alpha +e_i}\) is the weak derivative \(\partial _iJ_\alpha \) for \(\lvert \alpha \rvert {\lt}m\). The seminorm \(\lvert J\rvert _{H^m}\) sums the squared \(L^2\) norms over all ordered derivative words of length \(m\) (for \(m=2\) the Frobenius norm of the Hessian), and \(\lVert J\rVert _{H^m}^2=\sum _{j\le m}\lvert J\rvert _{H^j}^2\).

A convex polygon is a nonempty bounded finite intersection of open half-planes. A jet is in \(H^1_0(\Omega )\) if it is a weak jet of order one and an \(H^1\)-limit of smooth functions with compact support in \(\Omega \). A weak solution of \(-\Delta u=f\), \(u=0\) on \(\partial \Omega \), is a jet \(u\in H^1_0(\Omega )\) with \((\nabla u,\nabla v)_\Omega =(f,v)_\Omega \) for all smooth compactly supported \(v\).

A mesh consists of finitely many affine triangles \(K\), a list of edges \(e\) with an owner side and an optional neighbour side, and outward unit normals. It is admissible for \(\Omega \) with shape bound \(\kappa \) if the closed triangles cover \(\overline\Omega \), have disjoint interiors and meet in common sub-simplices, \(h_K\le \kappa \rho _K\) for an inscribed ball of radius \(\rho _K\), and the edge list matches the triangle sides, exactly the edges without neighbour lying on \(\partial \Omega \). \(h=\max _Kh_K\) and \(h_e\) is the length of \(e\).

A broken field has a jet on every triangle and a trace jet on every side. The traces of a jet on a triangle are Sobolev traces if they are \(L^2\) limits of the restrictions of smooth functions whose derivatives converge in \(L^2\) of the triangle; a broken field is broken \(H^2\) if every cell jet is an \(H^2\) jet with such traces. A broken field realizes a global jet \(u\) if its cell jets are those of \(u\). \(V_h\) consists of the piecewise polynomials of degree at most \(p\), and \(W_h=V_h+H^2(\Omega )\cap H^1_0(\Omega )\) of the broken fields that are sums of a discrete field and a field realizing an \(H^2\cap H^1_0\) jet.

With \(g(w,v)=(\nabla _hw,\nabla _hv)_\Omega \), \(C(w,v)=\sum _e\langle \{ \nabla _hw\} ,[\! [v]\! ]\rangle _e\), \(C^T(w,v)=C(v,w)\), \(D_\beta (w,v)=\sum _{e\text{ interior}}\langle [\! [\nabla _hw]\! ], \beta _e\cdot [\! [v]\! ]\rangle _e\), \(A_j(w,v)=\sum _e\eta _eh_e^{-1}\langle [\! [w]\! ],[\! [v]\! ]\rangle _e\), \(A_r(w,v)=\sum _e\eta _e(r_e([\! [w]\! ]),r_e([\! [v]\! ]))_\Omega \) and the superpenalties \(A_j^{\sup }\), \(A_r^{\sup }\) (weights \(h_e^{-(2p+1)}\) and \(h_e^{-2p}\)):

\begin{align*} B_{IP}& =g-C-C^T+A_j,\qquad B_{Bassi}=g-C-C^T+A_r,\\ B_{Brezzi}& =g-C-C^T+(R(\cdot ),R(\cdot ))+A_r,\\ B_{LDG}& =g-C-C^T-D_\beta -D_\beta ^T+(A_\beta (\cdot ),A_\beta (\cdot ))+A_j,\\ B_{NIPG}& =g-C+C^T+A_j,\qquad B_{BO}=g-C+C^T,\qquad B_{BR}=g-C-C^T+(R(\cdot ),R(\cdot )), \end{align*}

the pure penalties \(g+A_j\) and \(g+A_r\), and the superpenalized \(g+A_j^{\sup }\), \(g+A_r^{\sup }\), \(g-C+C^T+A_j^{\sup }\). The penalty threshold of the four standard methods is \(\eta _{\min }{\gt}\eta _*\) for IP, \(\eta _{\min }{\gt}3\) for Bassi et al., and none for Brezzi et al. and LDG.

For each of the nine methods and the three superpenalized methods, the numerical fluxes \(\hat u\), \(\hat\sigma \) of Table 3.1 of the paper. A pair \((u_h,\sigma _h)\in V_h\times \Sigma _h\) solves the flux formulation if on every triangle \(K\)

\[ (\sigma _h,\tau )_K=-(u_h,\operatorname {div}\tau )_K+\langle \hat u,\tau \cdot n_K\rangle _{\partial K},\qquad (\sigma _h,\nabla v)_K=(f,v)_K+\langle \hat\sigma \cdot n_K,v\rangle _{\partial K} \]

for all polynomial \(\tau \), \(v\) of degree at most \(p\). The eliminated auxiliary field is \(\nabla _hu_h+R(u_h)\), \(\nabla _hu_h+A_\beta (u_h)\), \(\nabla _hu_h-R(u_h)\) or \(\nabla _hu_h\), depending on the method.
