Let \(\Omega \) be a bounded open convex polygon. There is \(C_{reg}{\gt}0\) such that for every \(f\in L^2(\Omega )\) the problem \(-\Delta u=f\), \(u=0\) on \(\partial \Omega \) has a weak solution \(u\in H^1_0(\Omega )\), unique up to its derivatives of order at most one, and \(u\in H^2(\Omega )\) with \(-\Delta u=f\) and \(\lVert u\rVert _{H^2(\Omega )}\le C_{reg}\lVert f\rVert _{L^2(\Omega )}\). Every \(H^2(\Omega )\) jet has Sobolev traces on the edges of every admissible mesh.

Proof ▶

Existence and uniqueness by Lax–Milgram (Lemma 5.1); the Hessian and its bound by Theorem 5.8 and the energy estimate; the traces by Lemma 4.4.

Fix \(p\ge 1\) and \(\kappa {\gt}0\). There is \(\eta _*=\eta _*(p,\kappa ){\gt}0\) such that for every convex polygon \(\Omega \), all \(0{\lt}\eta _{\min }\le \eta _{\max }\), \(b\ge 0\) and every method among IP, Bassi et al., Brezzi et al. and LDG satisfying its threshold, there are \(C_b,C_s,C_E,C_0{\gt}0\) with the following properties for every admissible mesh, all \(\eta _e\in [\eta _{\min },\eta _{\max }]\) and all \(\lvert \beta _e\rvert \le b\):

  1. \(B_h\) is symmetric and bilinear on \(W_h\), \(\lvert B_h(w,v)\rvert \le C_bN_h(w)N_h(v)\), and \(B_h(v_h,v_h)\ge C_sN_h(v_h)^2\) on \(V_h\);

  2. for every \(f\in L^2(\Omega )\) the discrete problem has exactly one solution \(u_h\);

  3. if the weak solution \(u\) lies in \(H^{p+1}(\Omega )\), then \(N_h(u-u_h)\le C_Eh^p\lvert u\rvert _{H^{p+1}(\Omega )}\) and \(\lVert u-u_h\rVert _{L^2(\Omega )}\le C_0h^{p+1}\lvert u\rvert _{H^{p+1}(\Omega )}\).

Proof ▶

Boundedness and coercivity are Lemmas 7.3 and 7.4; solvability follows by Lemma 7.5. With consistency (Lemma 7.7), the abstract energy estimate (Lemma 7.8) and the interpolant of Lemma 6.4 give the energy bound. The \(L^2\) bound follows by duality (Lemma 7.9), using adjoint consistency and the \(H^2\) regularity of Theorem 3.1.

For each of the twelve methods and arbitrary \(\eta _e\), \(\beta _e\), a pair \((u_h,\sigma _h)\) solves the flux formulation if and only if \(\sigma _h\) is the eliminated auxiliary field of \(u_h\) and \(u_h\) solves the primal problem \(B_h(u_h,v_h)=(f,v_h)_\Omega \). Every flux solution satisfies the balance \(\int _Sf+\int _{\partial S}\hat\sigma \cdot n_S=0\) for every set \(S\) of triangles.

Proof ▶

The first equation of the flux formulation determines \(\sigma _h\) (Lemma 8.1); substituting it into the second gives the primal form. For the balance, sum the second equation with \(v=1\) over \(S\); the contributions of interior faces cancel.

Fix \(p\ge 1\), \(\kappa \), \(\Omega \) and \(0{\lt}\eta _{\min }\le \eta _{\max }\). With constants uniform over admissible meshes and \(\eta _e\in [\eta _{\min },\eta _{\max }]\):

  • NIPG is bounded on \(W_h\), coercive on \(V_h\) and uniquely solvable, and for \(u\in H^{p+1}(\Omega )\): \(N_h(u-u_h)\le C_Eh^p\lvert u\rvert _{H^{p+1}}\) and \(\lVert u-u_h\rVert _{L^2}\le C_Lh^p\lvert u\rvert _{H^{p+1}}\);

  • the pure penalty methods \(g+A_j\) and \(g+A_r\) are bounded, coercive and uniquely solvable;

  • the three superpenalized methods are uniquely solvable, and their errors satisfy \(h^p\) bounds in the superpenalty norms and \(h^{p+1}\) bounds in \(L^2\), in terms of \(\lVert u\rVert _{H^{p+1}}\) (of \(\lvert u\rvert _{H^{p+1}}\) for superpenalized NIPG).

Proof ▶

Stability is Lemma 9.1, solvability Lemma 7.5. For NIPG the energy estimate follows from consistency (Theorem 3.6) and Lemma 7.8; the \(L^2\) bound from the broken Poincaré inequality (Lemma 9.2). For the superpenalties the consistency defect \(C(u,\cdot )\) is of order \(h^p\) in the superpenalty norm by the jump control of Lemma 9.3, and Lemma 9.4 gives both estimates.

Let \(u\in H^2(\Omega )\cap H^1_0(\Omega )\) with \(-\Delta u=f\). For all \(v\in W_h\) and arbitrary \(\eta _e\): NIPG and superpenalized NIPG satisfy \(B(u,v)=(f,v)_\Omega \) and \(B(v,u)=(f,v)_\Omega +2C(u,v)\); the pure penalties and the pure superpenalties satisfy \(B(u,v)=B(v,u)=(f,v)_\Omega +C(u,v)\).

Proof ▶

The identity \(g(u,v)-C(u,v)=(f,v)_\Omega \) (Lemma 7.6) and the vanishing of the jumps of \(u\), which removes all penalty terms and \(C(v,u)\).

For \(p\ge 2\), every admissible mesh and every \(f\in L^2(\Omega )\) the Baumann–Oden problem has exactly one solution, and if \(u\in H^{p+1}(\Omega )\), then \(N_h(u-u_h)\le C_Eh^p\lvert u\rvert _{H^{p+1}}\) and \(\lVert u-u_h\rVert _{L^2}\le C_Lh^p\lvert u\rvert _{H^{p+1}}\), with uniform constants. For \(p=1\) there is a one-triangle mesh on which \(B_{BO}(w,v)=(\nabla w,\nabla v)_\Omega \) for affine \(w,v\), and on which the load \(f=1\) has no discrete solution.

Proof ▶

Stability and solvability are Lemma 10.1; the energy estimate uses the consistency of \(B_{BO}\) and a test with piecewise quadratics that control the jumps of piecewise constants. The counterexample is Lemma 10.2.

For \(p\ge 1\):

  • \(Z_h=\{ v_h\in V_h:P_{p-1}v_h=0\text{ on every triangle},\ \{ v_h\} =0\text{ on every interior edge}\} \);

  • the problem \(B_{BR}(u_h,v_h)=(f,v_h)_\Omega \) is solvable if and only if \(f\perp Z_h\); its solutions form one coset \(u_h+Z_h\), and \(\mathcal{D}_hu_h\) and \(P_{p-1}u_h\) are unique;

  • it is solvable for elementwise polynomial loads of degree at most \(p-1\), and then, for \(u\in H^{p+1}(\Omega )\), \(\lVert \nabla u-\mathcal{D}_hu_h\rVert \le C_\sigma h^p\lvert u\rvert _{H^{p+1}}\) and \(\lVert P_{p-1}(u-u_h)\rVert \le C_0h^{p+1}\lvert u\rvert _{H^{p+1}}\);

  • the full \(L^2\) error is not controlled: on a one-triangle mesh there is \(0\ne z\in Z_h\), every \(tz\) solves the problem with \(f=0\), and \(\lVert 0-tz\rVert =\lvert t\rvert \lVert z\rVert \).

Proof ▶

Lemmas 11.3, 11.4, 11.6 and 11.7.
