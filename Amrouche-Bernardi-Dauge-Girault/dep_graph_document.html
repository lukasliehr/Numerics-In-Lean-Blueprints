Definition 3.4Boundary fluxes and cut fluxes

Let \(v\in L^2(\Omega )^3\) with \(\operatorname {div}v=d\).

  • A cutoff for \(\Gamma _i\) is a smooth compactly supported \(\eta \) on \(\mathbb {R}^3\) that is \(1\) near \(\Gamma _i\) and \(0\) near the other components. \(F_i(v)=0\) means \(\int _\Omega v\cdot \nabla \eta +\int _\Omega d\, \eta =0\) for every cutoff \(\eta \) of \(\Gamma _i\), for all \(0\le i\le I\).

  • \(q\in H^1(\Omega ^{\circ })\) jumps by \(c\) across \(\Sigma _j\) if, in each ball of the patches of \(\Sigma _j\), the function equal to \(q-c\) on the minus side and to \(q\) on the plus side is the restriction of an \(H^1\) function. It has unit jump across \(\Sigma _j\) if it jumps by \(1\) across \(\Sigma _j\) and by \(0\) across the other cuts (the paper’s lifts \(r_j\)).

  • \(C_j(v)=0\) means \(\int _{\Omega ^{\circ }}v\cdot \nabla r+\int _{\Omega ^{\circ }}d\, r=0\) for every \(r\) with unit jump across \(\Sigma _j\), for all \(j\).

The conditions are imposed for every admissible cutoff and lift, so no choice is involved.

Definition 3.3Admissible geometric data

A graph patch is an open box, in coordinates given by an affine isometry of \(\mathbb {R}^3\), together with a Lipschitz function of the first two coordinates; it splits the box into the regions above and below the graph. A set has Lipschitz boundary if every boundary point lies in a patch in which the set is the region above the graph and its boundary is the graph. Geometric data of an open set \(\Omega \subset \mathbb {R}^3\) consist of:

  • the facts that \(\Omega \) is bounded, connected and has Lipschitz boundary;

  • the boundary components \(\Gamma _0,\dots ,\Gamma _I\): the connected components of \(\partial \Omega \), pairwise disjoint and covering \(\partial \Omega \), where \(\Gamma _0\) borders the unbounded component of \(\mathbb {R}^3\setminus \overline\Omega \);

  • a cutting system \(\Sigma _1,\dots ,\Sigma _J\) (numbered from \(0\) in Lean): connected subsets of \(\Omega \) with rims on \(\partial \Omega \) and pairwise disjoint closures. Each cut is covered by finitely many balls in which \(\Omega \setminus \Sigma _j\) splits into a connected Lipschitz minus side and a connected Lipschitz plus side, the cut being a smooth graph between them with coherent orientation. The cut domain \(\Omega ^{\circ }=\Omega \setminus \bigcup _j\Sigma _j\) is open, simply connected and pseudo-Lipschitz (near each boundary point it consists of one or two Lipschitz pieces).

No extension, Poincaré, trace, closed-range or potential theorem is part of these data.

Definition 5.1Analytic inputs

The analytic inputs for geometric data \(G\) of \(\Omega \) are the following statements.

  1. The cuts are Lebesgue null.

  2. If \(h\in L^2(\Omega )^3\) is curl free and divergence free, and \(v\in L^2(\Omega )^3\) satisfies \(v\cdot n=0\) and \(C_j(v)=0\) for all \(j\), then \((v,h)_{L^2(\Omega )^3}=0\).

  3. Every divergence-free \(u\) with \(F_i(u)=0\) for all \(i\) is a curl.

  4. A divergence-free \(\psi \) with \(\psi \times n=0\) (for \(\operatorname {curl}\psi =0\)) and \(F_i(\psi )=0\) for all \(i\) vanishes.

  5. If \(\psi \times n=0\) for a field \(\psi \) with \(\operatorname {curl}\psi =u\), then \(u\cdot n=0\).

  6. The gradient of a function with unit jump across a cut, extended across the null cuts, is curl free on \(\Omega \).

  7. If \(\psi \times n=0\) for a field \(\psi \) with \(\operatorname {curl}\psi =u\), and \(u\cdot n=0\), then \(C_j(u)=0\) for all \(j\).

Definition 3.5Tangential and normal potentials

Let \(u,\psi \in L^2(\Omega )^3\). Then \(\psi \) is a tangential potential of \(u\) if \(\operatorname {curl}\psi =u\), \(\operatorname {div}\psi =0\), \(\psi \cdot n=0\) and \(C_j(\psi )=0\) for all \(j\), and a normal potential of \(u\) if \(\operatorname {curl}\psi =u\), \(\operatorname {div}\psi =0\), \(\psi \times n=0\) and \(F_i(\psi )=0\) for all \(i\). In Showcase.lean the theorems are written with the scoped notation of the paper (namespace VP.Paper), which abbreviates exactly these conditions; the file checks each abbreviation by rfl.

Definition 3.1Fields, weak derivatives and Sobolev spaces

Let \(\Omega \subset \mathbb {R}^n\) be open. \(L^2(\Omega )\) and \(L^2(\Omega )^n\) are the square-integrable functions and vector fields, as classes modulo equality almost everywhere; \(C_c^\infty (\Omega )\) and \(C_c^\infty (\Omega )^3\) are the smooth compactly supported test functions and test fields. For \(v,c\in L^2(\Omega )^3\) and \(d\in L^2(\Omega )\):

  • \(\operatorname {div}v=d\) if \(\int _\Omega v\cdot \nabla \varphi =-\int _\Omega d\, \varphi \) for all \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty (\Omega )\); \(v\) is divergence free if \(\operatorname {div}v=0\);

  • \(\operatorname {curl}v=c\) if \(\int _\Omega v\cdot \operatorname {curl}\varphi =\int _\Omega c\cdot \varphi \) for all \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty (\Omega )^3\);

  • an element of \(H^1(\Omega )\) is a pair of \(q\in L^2(\Omega )\) and its weak gradient \(\nabla q\in L^2(\Omega )^n\); an element of \(H^1(\Omega )^3\) is a pair of a field and its weak Jacobian in \(L^2\).

Definition 3.2Zero normal and tangential traces

Let \(v,c\in L^2(\Omega )^3\) and \(d\in L^2(\Omega )\).

  • For a field with \(\operatorname {div}v=d\), \(v\cdot n=0\) on \(\partial \Omega \) means \(\int _\Omega v\cdot \nabla q+\int _\Omega d\, q=0\) for all \(q\in H^1(\Omega )\).

  • For a field with \(\operatorname {curl}v=c\), \(v\times n=0\) on \(\partial \Omega \) means \(\int _\Omega v\cdot \operatorname {curl}w-\int _\Omega c\cdot w=0\) for all \(w\in H^1(\Omega )^3\).

These are the paper’s weak definitions by Green’s formulas; no trace operator is needed.

Lemma 9.4The reflection estimate

Let \(u\) be divergence free on \(\Omega \), \(C\) a chart with exterior part \(S\) and Lipschitz constant \(K\), \(\zeta \) a smooth cutoff equal to \(1\) on the half box and supported in the box with \(\lvert \nabla \zeta \rvert \le C_\zeta \), and \(F\) smooth with support in the half box. Then

\[ \Bigl\lvert \int _\Omega u\cdot \nabla F\Bigr\rvert \le \lVert u\rVert _{L^2(\Omega )^3}\bigl(C_\zeta \lVert F\rVert _{L^2(S)}+(3+2K)\lVert \nabla F\rVert _{L^2(S)}\bigr). \]

Lemma 9.3Boundary charts and Lipschitz calculus

Every boundary point has arbitrarily small charts: a box \(V\) in frame coordinates in which \(\Omega \) lies strictly above a globally Lipschitz graph and the exterior strictly below it. The exterior part \(S=V\setminus \overline\Omega \) is star shaped with respect to its bottom slab, and the reflection across the graph is a Lipschitz, measure-preserving involution exchanging the two sides. A Lipschitz function on \(\mathbb {R}^3\) has its almost-everywhere gradient as weak gradient, and a Lipschitz function vanishing on the zero set of a continuous function \(\Phi \) stays Lipschitz after being cut off to \(\{ \Phi {\gt}0\} \).
