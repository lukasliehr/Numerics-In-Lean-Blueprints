Section 8 of Showcase.lean states each numbered result of the paper as a definition named after it (Proposition_3_2, …), with the paper’s quantifiers in the paper’s order and in the paper’s notation; Section 9 asserts them for \(d\in \{ 2,3\} \), \(k\ge 1\) and \(\rho \ge 1\). Propositions 3.2 (including its clause for \(s=1\)), 3.3 and 3.4, Theorem 3.5 and Corollary 3.7, as stated in the paper. Theorem 3.5 and Corollary 3.7 hold for every streamline parameter \(\tau {\gt}0\); the inf-sup condition (3.24) is written as: for every \(\nu \ne 0\) in \(V_h\) there is \(\mu \ne 0\) in \(V_h\) with \(B^{\mathrm{DG}}(\nu ,\mu )/(\lVert \nu \rVert _{\mathrm{SDG}}\lVert \mu \rVert _{\mathrm{SDG}})\ge \beta _{\mathrm{DG}}\).

Proof ▶

Each result is the corresponding stability or error estimate of the later chapters, translated from piecewise polynomials to the broken fields of \(V_h\). The inf-sup quotient follows from the normalized witness \(\lVert \mu \rVert _{\mathrm{SDG}}\le \lVert \nu \rVert _{\mathrm{SDG}}\), \(B^{\mathrm{DG}}(\nu ,\mu )\ge \beta \lVert \nu \rVert ^2_{\mathrm{SDG}}\), since the norm is definite on \(V_h\). The DG error estimate needs only DG continuity and the DG inf-sup condition, so it holds for every \(\tau {\gt}0\).

The exact solution satisfies the DG and SDG equations; the problems (3.2), (3.13), (4.1) and (4.4) have unique solutions; every weak \(H^m\) jet on a cell has unique traces, and every weak \(H^{k+1}(\Omega )\) jet is realized by a broken \(H^2\) field. The last fact shows that the error estimates apply to every solution \(\varphi \in H^{k+1}(\Omega )\).

Proof ▶

The local problems do not involve \(\tau \); their unique solvability is taken at \(\tau =1\), where the local inf-sup condition is available.

For \(s=1\) and every \(\varepsilon {\gt}0\) there is \(c{\gt}0\) with \(B^{\mathrm{DG}}(\mu ,\mu )\ge c\lVert \mu \rVert ^2_{\mathrm{DG}}\) on \(V_h\), uniformly in the admissible mesh, \(\kappa {\gt}0\) and \(a\).

Proof ▶

For \(s=1\) the consistency and adjoint terms cancel in \(B^{\mathrm{DG}}_D(\mu ,\mu )\). The convective part gives \(\tfrac 12\) of the jump energy by integration by parts on each cell.
