Lemma 6.1 Lagrange interpolation on a simplex
✓

Let the principal lattice of degree \(k\) have nodes \(x_\lambda \), and let the Lagrange basis be

\[ \varphi _\lambda =\prod _i\prod _{j{\lt}\lambda _i}\frac{k b_i-j}{\lambda _i-j}, \]

where \(b_i\) are the barycentric coordinates. Then \(\varphi _\lambda (x_\mu )=\delta _{\lambda \mu }\) and the interpolant reproduces polynomials of degree at most \(k\). Moreover \(\lvert \varphi _\lambda \rvert \le k^k\) on the cell.

Proof ▶

The nodal evaluation map is onto. Both spaces have dimension \(\binom {d+k}{k}\), so it is also one-to-one.

Lemma 6.2 Face conformity

If two cells share a facet, the interpolants of two functions that agree on the facet coincide on it. Hence cellwise interpolants of compatible functions form a coarse field in \(\overline{V}_h\).

Proof ▶

The vertices of a shared facet are extreme points of both convex hulls, so the facet determines a permutation of the vertex indices. Under this permutation the barycentric coordinates agree on the facet. Basis functions with \(\lambda _j\ge 1\) vanish on facet \(j\); the remaining lattice nodes lie on the facet.

Theorem 6.3 Continuous representatives

Let \(d\le 3\), and let \(J\) be a jet with \(H^2\) traces on a cell. The smooth approximants of \(J\) converge uniformly on the closed cell. The limit \(w\) is continuous, agrees a.e. with \(J\) and with its facet traces, and satisfies the sup bound against every smooth comparison function. For a global jet, the representatives of neighbouring cells agree everywhere on the common face.

Proof ▶

The sup bound makes the approximants uniformly Cauchy. On a face, the two representatives agree a.e. by trace agreement. The face measure charges every relatively open subset of the face (homothety argument), so a.e. equality of continuous functions is equality.

For \(d\le 3\) there is \(C_I\) with the following property. For every exact solution there is a coarse field \(c\in \overline{V}_h\) with \(\sum _T\lVert \varphi -c\rVert ^2_{\mathrm{SDG}(T)}\le C_I^2\sum _T(\lvert a\rvert h_T^{2k+1}+\kappa h_T^{2k})\lvert \varphi \rvert ^2_{H^{k+1}(T)}\).

Proof ▶

Take \(c|_T=I_Tw_T\), the Lagrange interpolant of the continuous representative. With a Bramble–Hilbert polynomial \(q\) we have \(I_Tw-q=I_T(w-q)\). Its nodal values are bounded by the sup bound, so the inverse inequalities give the cell terms. The facet terms follow from the sup bound and \(\lvert F_j\rvert /\lvert T\rvert \le d\rho /(2h_T)\).
