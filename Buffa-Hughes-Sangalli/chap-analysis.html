Lemma 6.1 Slicing and the cone formula
✓
#

Uses

  • Theorem 2.4
  • Theorem 2.5

Lean declarations

  • BHS.integral_eq_mul_integral_slices
  • BHS.integral_closedInterior_cone
  • BHS.integral_radial_divergence

Integrals over a simplex can be computed along slices parallel to a facet, and as a cone over a facet with apex at the opposite vertex. Applied to the radial field, this gives the divergence identity for the radial vector field.

Proof ▶

Fubini along a direction, and the change of variables of a dilation.

Theorem 6.2 Divergence theorem on simplices
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.1
  • Definition 2.2
  • Lemma 6.1
  • Lemma 5.2

Lean declarations

  • BHS.integral_fderiv_eq_sum_facets
  • BHS.integral_inner_gradient_cell

For \(C^1\) fields \(F\) on a neighbourhood of a simplex, \(\int _T\operatorname {div}F=\sum _j\int _{F_j}F\cdot n_j\, \mathrm{d}\mathcal H^{d-1}\). In particular, Green’s formula holds for smooth functions on the cells.

Proof ▶

Reduce to a single coordinate direction and integrate along slices; the facet integrals are identified with the \((d-1)\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

Lemma 6.3 Polynomial inverse and trace inequalities
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.3
  • Lemma 5.1
  • Theorem 6.2

Lean declarations

  • BHS.exists_inverse_ball_sandwich
  • BHS.exists_cell_inverse
  • BHS.exists_cell_inverse_H2
  • BHS.exists_cell_trace
  • BHS.exists_cell_gradient_trace

For polynomials \(p\) of degree at most \(k\) on cells of \(\rho \)-regular meshes,

\[ h_T^2\lVert \partial _ip\rVert ^2_{L^2(T)}\le C\lVert p\rVert ^2_{L^2(T)},\qquad h_T^2\lvert p\rvert ^2_{H^2(T)}\le C\lvert p\rvert ^2_{H^1(T)},\qquad h_T\lVert p\rVert ^2_{L^2(F_j)}\le C\lVert p\rVert ^2_{L^2(T)}, \]

with \(C=C(d,k,\rho )\).

Proof ▶

Equivalence of norms on the finite-dimensional space of polynomials on the unit ball. Then scale between the inscribed and the circumscribed ball.

Theorem 6.5 Density of smooth functions
✓
#

Uses

  • Theorem 2.6

Lean declarations

  • BHS.exists_smooth_approx

On an open convex set containing a ball, every weak \(H^m\) jet is the limit of smooth functions in all derivatives of order at most \(m\).

Proof ▶

Dilate towards the centre of the ball, so that the dilated function is defined on a neighbourhood of the closure. Then mollify; weak derivatives commute with both operations.

Theorem 6.6 Traces exist, are unique, and agree across faces
✓
#

Uses

  • Theorem 2.7
  • Theorem 6.5
  • Lemma 6.4
  • Lemma 5.3

Lean declarations

  • BHS.exists_hasSobolevTrace
  • BHS.hasSobolevTrace_unique
  • BHS.localTraceTheory
  • BHS.traceRealizationExists
  • BHS.traceAgreement

Every weak \(H^m\) jet on a cell (\(m\ge 1\)) has traces of order \({\lt}m\) on its facets, unique almost everywhere. Every weak \(H^{k+1}(\Omega )\) jet is realized by a broken \(H^2\) field. For a global weak jet, the traces of the two cells sharing an interior face agree almost everywhere.

Proof ▶

Existence and uniqueness: the trace inequality makes the facet restrictions of the smooth approximants Cauchy in \(L^2\). Agreement: Green’s formula on both cells and the weak derivative on \(\Omega \) give \(\int _e(t_T-t_S)\psi =0\) for smooth \(\psi \) supported in \(\operatorname {int}(T\cup S)\) away from the other facets. The fundamental lemma for the face measure gives \(t_T=t_S\) a.e. there, and the rest of \(e\) is \(\mathcal H^{d-1}\)-null.

Lemma 6.8 Poincaré–Wirtinger and polynomial moments
✓
#

Uses

  • Theorem 2.5
  • Theorem 6.5

Lean declarations

  • BHS.poincare_convex
  • BHS.poincare_weakJet
  • BHS.exists_poly_moments

On a compact convex set \(K\) of diameter \(h\), \(\int _K\lvert u-\bar u\rvert ^2\le C h^2\int _K\lvert \nabla u\rvert ^2\), with \(C\) depending only on \(d\) and \(h^d/\lvert K\rvert \). This holds for \(C^1\) functions and for weak \(H^1\) jets. For given moments \(m_\alpha \), \(\lvert \alpha \rvert \le k\), there is a polynomial \(q\) of degree at most \(k\) with \(\int \partial ^\alpha q=m_\alpha \).

Proof ▶

Write \(u(x)-u(y)\) as an integral along the segment and change variables; moments are fixed from the highest order downwards.

Theorem 6.9 Bramble–Hilbert
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 6.8
  • Lemma 5.1

Lean declarations

  • BHS.exists_brambleHilbert

There is \(C=C(d,k)\) such that for every cell of an admissible mesh and every weak \(H^{k+1}\) jet \(J\) on it, some polynomial \(q\) of degree at most \(k\) satisfies \(h_T^{2j}\lvert J-q\rvert ^2_{H^j(T)}\le C\, h_T^{2(k+1)}\lvert J\rvert ^2_{H^{k+1}(T)}\) for all \(j\le k+1\).

Proof ▶

Choose \(q\) so that all cell averages of \(\partial ^\alpha (J-q)\), \(\lvert \alpha \rvert \le k\), vanish. Then iterate the weak Poincaré inequality.

Theorem 6.10 Scaled sup bound, \(d\le 3\)
✓
#

Uses

  • Theorem 2.5
  • Lemma 5.1

Lean declarations

  • BHS.exists_sobolev_sup
  • BHS.sup_bound_convex

For \(d\le 3\) and smooth \(u\), at every point \(x\) of a cell, \(\lvert T\rvert \, u(x)^2\le C\bigl(\lVert u\rVert ^2_{L^2(T)}+h_T^2\lvert u\rvert ^2_{H^1(T)}+h_T^4\lvert u\rvert ^2_{H^2(T)}\bigr)\) with \(C=3(4d+1)\).

Proof ▶

Average Taylor’s formula with integral remainder over the cell. The remainder is controlled after the substitution \(s=\sigma ^2\), which needs \(d\le 3\).
