Definition 2.1 Admissible meshes
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • BHSShowcase.MeshData
  • BHSShowcase.AdmissibleMesh
  • BHSShowcase.cellSet
  • BHSShowcase.facet
  • BHSShowcase.faceThickness

A mesh consists of finitely many affine \(d\)-simplices \(T\), a list of faces with an owner facet and an optional neighbour facet, and outward unit normals. It is admissible with shape constant \(\rho \ge 1\) if:

  • the domain \(\Omega \) is bounded, connected, Lipschitz, and equal to the interior of the union of the cells;

  • the cells have disjoint interiors and meet in common subsimplices (conformity);

  • each cell contains a ball of radius \(r\) with \(\operatorname {diam}T\le \rho r\);

  • each facet belongs to exactly one face;

  • exactly the faces without a neighbour lie on \(\partial \Omega \).

The face thickness is \(h_{\perp }=(\lvert T^+\rvert +\lvert T^-\rvert )/(2\lvert e\rvert )\) on interior faces and \(\lvert T\rvert /\lvert e\rvert \) on boundary faces.

Definition 2.2 Weak jets and Sobolev traces
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • BHSShowcase.WeakJet
  • BHSShowcase.HasSobolevTrace
  • BHSShowcase.BrokenField
  • BHSShowcase.BrokenH2
  • BHSShowcase.RealizesGlobalJet

A jet assigns a function \(J_\alpha \) to each multi-index \(\alpha \).

  • It is a weak \(H^m(\Omega )\) jet if \(J_\alpha \in L^2(\Omega )\) for \(\lvert \alpha \rvert \le m\) and \(J_{\alpha +e_i}\) is the distributional derivative \(\partial _i J_\alpha \) for \(\lvert \alpha \rvert {\lt}m\).

  • Traces on the facets of a cell are \(L^2\) limits of the facet restrictions of smooth functions whose derivatives converge in \(L^2\) of the cell (HasSobolevTrace).

  • A broken field has one jet per cell and one trace jet per facet. It is broken \(H^2\) if every cell jet is \(H^2\) with these traces.

For \(s\in \{ -1,0,1\} \) (symmetric, neutral, skew), the DG form is

\begin{align*} B^{\mathrm{DG}}(\nu ,\mu ) & = \sum _T\int _T(\kappa \nabla \nu \cdot \nabla \mu -\nu \, a\cdot \nabla \mu ) +\sum _{e\in \mathcal{E}_h^{o}}\int _e\bigl([\! [\mu ]\! ]\cdot (a\nu ^- - \kappa \nabla \nu ^-) + s\kappa \nabla \mu ^-\cdot [\! [\nu ]\! ]\bigr)\\ & \quad +\sum _{e\subset \partial \Omega }\int _e\bigl(s\kappa \nabla \mu \cdot n\, \nu -\kappa \nabla \nu \cdot n\, \mu + (a\cdot n)^+\mu \nu \bigr) +\varepsilon \sum _{e}\int _e\frac{\kappa }{h_{\perp }}[\! [\mu ]\! ]\cdot [\! [\nu ]\! ], \end{align*}

with upwind values \(\nu ^-\) and the matching load \(L^{\mathrm{DG}}\). The SDG form adds \(\sum _T\tau _T\int _T(-\kappa \Delta \nu +a\cdot \nabla \nu )(a\cdot \nabla \mu )\), with \(\tau _T=\tau \min (h_T/\lvert a\rvert , h_T^2/\kappa )\). The norms are

\begin{align*} \lVert \nu \rVert _{\mathrm{DG}}^2& =\sum _T\kappa \bigl(\lvert \nu \rvert _{H^1(T)}^2+h_T^2\lvert \nu \rvert _{H^2(T)}^2\bigr) +\varepsilon \sum _e\frac{\kappa }{h_{\perp }}\lVert [\! [\nu ]\! ]\rVert _{L^2(e)}^2 +\sum _e\lVert \lvert a\cdot n\rvert ^{1/2}[\! [\nu ]\! ]\rVert _{L^2(e)}^2,\\ \lVert \nu \rVert _{\mathrm{SDG}}^2& =\lVert \nu \rVert _{\mathrm{DG}}^2+\sum _T\tau _T\lVert a\cdot \nabla \nu \rVert _{L^2(T)}^2,\\ |\! |\! |\nu |\! |\! |_{\mathrm{SDG}}^2& =\lVert \nu \rVert _{\mathrm{SDG}}^2+\sum _{e\in \mathcal{E}_h^{o}}\lVert \lvert a\cdot n\rvert ^{1/2}\nu ^-\rVert ^2_{L^2(e)} +\sum _T\tau _T^{-1}\lVert \nu \rVert ^2_{L^2(T)}. \end{align*}

On a cell \(T\), with perturbation \(\delta \ge 0\),

\begin{align*} b_T(\nu ,\mu )& =\int _T(\kappa \nabla \nu \cdot \nabla \mu -\nu \, a\cdot \nabla \mu )\\ & \quad +\sum _{j}\int _{F_j}\Bigl(-\kappa \nabla \nu \cdot n\, \mu +s\kappa \nabla \mu \cdot n\, \nu +\bigl(\varepsilon \tfrac {\kappa }{h_{\perp }}+(1+\delta )(a\cdot n)^+\bigr)\nu \mu \Bigr). \end{align*}

Let \(\ell _T(\bar\nu ,f;\mu )\) be the load with boundary data \(\bar\nu \). The local energy \(\lVert \nu \rVert ^2_{\mathrm{SDG}(T)}\) is defined as in (4.7) of the paper. For a continuous piecewise-polynomial coarse field \(\bar\nu \in \overline{V}_h\), the transfer \(\mathfrak {T}_h(\bar\nu ,f)\) is the solution of \(b_T(\nu ,\mu )=\ell _T(\bar\nu ,f;\mu )\) on all cells. The trial space is \(\mathfrak {T}_h(\overline{V}_h,f)\).

The DG, SDG and SMDG solutions are defined by the Galerkin equations (3.2), (3.13) and (4.4). An exact solution is a weak \(H^{k+1}(\Omega )\) jet with \(L^2\) data, realized by a broken field with traces, satisfying the equation a.e. in each cell and the boundary condition a.e. on \(\partial \Omega \). The error indicator is \(\bigl(\sum _T(\lvert a\rvert h_T^{2k+1}+\kappa h_T^{2k})\lvert \varphi \rvert ^2_{H^{k+1}(T)}\bigr)^{1/2}\).
